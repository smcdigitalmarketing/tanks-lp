# Brand concepts

Three directions for the same product, built so that only the brand changes: same photos, same facts (`app/content/product.ts`), same prices (`app/content/offers.ts`), same seven-section page. Each concept is one file in `app/content/concepts/` plus one `[data-theme]` block in `app/app.css`.

Names are **working names**. None has had a trademark, domain or social-handle check.

## A · Hallie: Soft Everyday Comfort

**Feeling:** a comfortable everyday essential that makes getting dressed easier.
**Positioning:** for women who are done with hooks and digging straps, sold with warmth and reassurance rather than hype.
**Hypothesis:** reassurance sells to a cautious, older buyer (the competitor's actual audience): a warm voice, visible sizing help and a gentle fit guarantee convert better than urgency.

| Colour     | Hex       | Use                 |
| ---------- | --------- | ------------------- |
| Cream      | `#FBF6EF` | Page background     |
| Muted rose | `#B4677A` | Buttons, accents    |
| Blush      | `#F5E2DE` | Savings, highlights |
| Oat        | `#F3E9DF` | Alternate sections  |
| Cocoa      | `#3B2F2C` | Text, footer        |

**Wordmark:** lowercase "hallie" in DM Serif Display with a rose full stop.
**Voice:** warm, personal, plain-spoken. Second person, short sentences, the small details of an ordinary day; never gushing.

> "A soft tank with the support already inside. Pull it on in the morning and get on with your day: no hooks to reach for, no wire, no band digging in by lunchtime."

**Early Black Friday:** "Stock up on your everyday tank, a little early." The bar turns dark cocoa with blush text; the 5-pack leads.
**Why it might win:** closest to the buyer the competitor already converts, but without the fake urgency, so it's the safest bet on trust.
**Risk:** can read as "nice" rather than distinctive; rose and cream are common in the category.

## B · Oriel: Modern Premium Essentials

**Feeling:** an elevated wardrobe essential designed around comfort.
**Positioning:** the built-in bra tank as a considered design piece, sold on line, fabric feel and simplicity rather than on discounts.
**Hypothesis:** a premium, editorial presentation lets the same tank hold its price: buyers who see a design piece rather than a deal convert on singles and sets without heavy discount messaging.

| Colour   | Hex       | Use                |
| -------- | --------- | ------------------ |
| Ivory    | `#F7F4EE` | Page background    |
| Charcoal | `#232323` | Text, buttons      |
| Taupe    | `#8C7B6B` | Accents, labels    |
| Stone    | `#ECE6DC` | Alternate sections |
| Linen    | `#FFFDF9` | Cards              |

**Wordmark:** "ORIEL" in widely spaced Cormorant capitals over a hairline, with "ESSENTIALS" in small Inter caps.
**Voice:** spare, assured, editorial. Short declarative lines and design language (line, layer, cut); no exclamation marks; "colourways", "sets".

> "A refined tank with a built-in bra. No wire, no hooks, no band: a single clean layer, worn alone or beneath everything else."

**Early Black Friday:** "The Support Tank, at its Black Friday price." The bar goes near-black with stone text; the copy says "early access to seasonal pricing", with no shouting.
**Why it might win:** it stands furthest from the competitor's look, which matters if the competitor's reputation (3.2★) spreads.
**Risk:** the photos are the weakest fit here. Premium presentation exposes AI-looking 1024 px images most, so this concept depends most on a real shoot. The audience may also be more price-driven than the look suggests.

## C · Daywell: Contemporary Comfortwear

**Feeling:** everyday clothing that works harder and feels better.
**Positioning:** a modern DTC comfortwear brand. The bra tank is a practical upgrade, one piece that replaces two, sold with direct copy, clear numbers and strong product photography.
**Hypothesis:** a direct, practical DTC pitch (one piece instead of two, clear pack maths) moves buyers to the 3- and 5-packs faster than an emotional or premium story.

| Colour     | Hex       | Use                |
| ---------- | --------- | ------------------ |
| White      | `#FFFFFF` | Page background    |
| Warm grey  | `#F2EFEB` | Alternate sections |
| Deep plum  | `#5A2A4E` | Buttons, accents   |
| Terracotta | `#C2603F` | Highlights, stars  |
| Ink        | `#1B1720` | Text, footer       |

**Wordmark:** lowercase "daywell" in bold Space Grotesk beside a plum tile with a rising-sun mark.
**Voice:** direct, practical, confident. Short lines, numbers, lists. Says what it does and stops.

> "Two layers. Now one."

**Early Black Friday:** "Black Friday pricing, live now", and the bar spells out "5 tanks for $79.95 ($15.99 each)". Pack columns make the maths obvious.
**Why it might win:** clearest offer and best fit for paid social and younger buyers; the pack-column picker makes bigger bundles feel normal.
**Risk:** the most "DTC template" of the three. It works hardest on the offer, so it is the most exposed if the real Black Friday discount is smaller than the provisional one.

### D · Juno: Colour-block Pop

**Positioning:** a bold, playful comfortwear brand that sells the thirteen colours as much as the comfort.
**Hypothesis:** making colour the hero (mix any shades in a pack) turns a functional purchase into a fun one, lifting 3- and 5-pack share and standing out in a beige category.
**Palette:** Butter `#FFF6DC`, Tomato `#E5412E` (buttons), Cobalt `#2546D8` (bars), Sky `#DCE3FF` (savings), Ink `#16141F`.
**Wordmark:** lowercase "juno" in extra-bold Bricolage beside an overlapping tomato circle and cobalt square.
**Voice:** punchy and witty. "Ditch the hooks. Keep the support." "Hooks? Not invited."
**Risk:** the loudest of the six; may feel young for the competitor's 55+ buyer.

### E · Linnea: Scandinavian Calm

**Positioning:** a quiet base layer, sold on ease and simplicity with natural tones and space.
**Hypothesis:** a calm, low-pressure page builds trust with buyers tired of hard-sell bra marketing; strongest on singles and sets of three.
**Palette:** Birch `#F6F4EF`, Oat `#ECEAE3`, Sage `#7C8F7A`, Mist `#E3EADF` (savings), Slate `#2B3230` (text, buttons).
**Wordmark:** "linnea" in widely spaced light Hanken Grotesk beside a small sage sprig.
**Voice:** calm and unhurried. "A bra, quietly built in." "A calm Black Friday, a little early."
**Risk:** the quietest offer presentation; may undersell the bundles.

### F · Mabel & Co.: Vintage Heritage

**Positioning:** a warm, vintage-inspired label that tells the tank as an old-fashioned good idea, with a wry sense of humour.
**Hypothesis:** storytelling and personality make the tank memorable and suit buyers who want comfort without clinical or hard-sell messaging.
**Palette:** Cream `#F8F0E0`, Mustard `#F1CE6A` (savings), Burnt orange `#B5532A` (bars, offer blocks), Forest `#2F5A3E` (buttons), Espresso `#2A2418`.
**Wordmark:** "Mabel" in italic Fraunces, a burnt-orange ampersand, and "Co." in spaced Work Sans capitals.
**Voice:** warm, nostalgic, wry. "Whatever happened to simple, comfortable underthings?" "Straps with good manners."
**Risk:** "heritage" can read as old-fashioned for younger buyers; the name needs the same trademark checks as the others.

## What stays the same (on purpose)

- Photos: the same file in the same slot on every page and gallery slide.
- Facts, FAQ answers, size guide, comparison rows, guarantee wording and review quotes.
- Prices, bundles and savings maths.
- Section order (the SOP's seven sections) and the gallery order (the brief's seven slides plus the demo).
- No aggregate rating, no countdowns, no stock or scarcity copy, and every unverified item flagged.

## How to review

1. Start at `/`: the three cards and the side-by-side table.
2. Open each product page on a phone first (most traffic will be mobile), then desktop.
3. Use the dark strip at the top of any page to jump between A / B / C, product page / advertorial, and Standard / Early Black Friday, without losing your place. Switch "Flags" off to see the page as a shopper would.
4. Compare the gallery at `/gallery`: same slides, three brands.
